Etiology — Broad Classification

Atherosclerotic
major cause
Plaque formation in large vessels (ICA, MCA origin). Risk factors: HTN, DM, dyslipidemia, smoking. Leads to thrombotic or artery-to-artery embolic strokes.
Non-Atherosclerotic
arterial — various
Vasculitis, Fabry's disease, Fibromuscular dysplasia (FMD), Carotid artery dissection, Takayasu arteritis, Moya-moya disease ("puff of smoke" — multiple collaterals, ICA vasculopathy).

Arterial
Accounts for the vast majority of strokes. Divided into ischemic (85%) and hemorrhagic (15%). Arterial occlusion → territory infarction.
```
Ischaemic (85%)
Most common. Arterial occlusion leads to penumbra + core. Subtypes: Thrombotic, Embolic, Lacunar. MRI > CT early on.
Thrombotic
large vessel
In-situ clot at atherosclerotic plaque. Large vessel stroke. Evolving / stroke-in-evolution: progressive weakness over 48–72h. Often preceded by TIAs. Fibrin clot.
Embolic
m/c MCA stroke
Hyperacute presentation. Cortical features (+). Rapid recovery possible. Distal vessel involvement. Red clot. Hemorrhagic transformation common. LOC/seizures at onset.
Artery → Artery
m/c stroke overall
Embolus from neck vessels (carotid plaque) → MCA. Most common stroke mechanism overall. Artery-to-artery embolism.
Cardioembolic
seen in ↓
Mitral stenosis · Atrial fibrillation · Prosthetic valve · Dilated cardiomyopathy · Recent MI (within 4–6 wks) · Sick sinus syndrome. LA thrombus → cerebral embolus.
Lacunar
30–300 μm vessels
Lipohyalinosis of small perforating arteries (lenticulostriate). 3mm–2cm infarct. RF: HTN (elderly F>M). No cortical signs. CT: hypodense, MRI DWI best.
Pure Motor (m/c)
PLIC · lenticulostriate a.
Face+arm+leg equally weak. Posterior limb of internal capsule. Lenticulostriate artery. No sensory loss, no aphasia, no hemianopia.
Pure Sensory
VPL nucleus · thalamogeniculate a.
Hemisensory loss without weakness. Thalamogeniculate artery → VPL nucleus of thalamus.
Ataxic Hemiparesis
basi pons · basilar a.
CPC damage → C/L cerebellar findings. CS damage → C/L weakness. DRTC pathway involved. Basi pons lesion.
Dysarthria-Clumsy Hand
basi pons
Same mechanism as ataxic hemiparesis. Basilar artery perforators → basi pons. Dysarthria + hand clumsiness without significant limb weakness.
Haemorrhagic (15%)
Mx by neurosurgeon. Presents with severe headache ("thunderclap"), vomiting, neck stiffness (SAH). CT: hyperdense lesion acutely.
SAH
subarachnoid
Subarachnoid hemorrhage. Berry aneurysm rupture most common. "Worst headache of life." CT: blood in basal cisterns. LP: xanthochromia if CT -ve.
EDH
extra-dural
Extra-dural hematoma. Middle meningeal artery rupture. Lucid interval → deterioration. Biconvex (lenticular) hyperdensity on CT.
IVH
intraventricular
Intraventricular hemorrhage. Often extension of hypertensive ICH. Blood in ventricles → hydrocephalus. Very poor prognosis.
Venous (CVT)
cerebral venous thrombosis
Cerebral venous thrombosis. Symptoms: ↑ICT (headache, vomiting, seizures), patchy weakness. Imaging: "empty delta sign" on contrast CT.
Inherited
Factor V Leiden mutation (most common inherited thrombophilia). Also: Prothrombin G20210A, Protein C/S deficiency, AT-III deficiency.
Acquired
Antiphospholipid syndrome (APLA). Pregnancy/postpartum (most common acquired cause). OCP use, malignancy, infection (otitis → sigmoid sinus).

White Matter Conditions

CADASIL
NOTCH3 mutation
Cerebral Autosomal Dominant Arteriopathy with Subcortical Infarcts & Leucoencephalopathy. Recurrent lacunar strokes, dementia, migraine. Subcortical white matter lesions.
Cerebral Amyloid Angiopathy
microbleeds
Aβ deposition in vessel walls. Cortical microbleeds on GRE/SWI MRI. Lobar hemorrhages in elderly. Increased risk with anticoagulation.
```
MCA STROKE
~70% of all strokes · lateral cortex + BG + IC
Middle cerebral artery: M1 (sphenoidal), M2 (insular), M3 (opercular), M4 (cortical). Lenticulostriate perforators arise from M1. Dominant supply to motor, sensory, language cortex.

MCA Stroke Types by Territory

```
Total MCA Infarct
M1 occlusion
Complete MCA territory. Massive edema, midline shift. Contralateral hemiplegia + hemianesthesia + hemianopia + gaze deviation + aphasia/neglect. High mortality.
Malignant MCA Syndrome
≥50% territory, ≤60y
Decompressive hemicraniectomy ≤48h. DECIMAL trial: mortality 78% → 29%. NNT ~2. Indicated in age ≤60, NIHSS >15, DWI >145 cm².
Superior Division
frontal + parietal operculum
Upper MCA branches. Face+arm > leg weakness. Broca's aphasia (dominant side). Usually embolic. No visual field defect typically.
Broca's Aphasia
non-fluent · comprehension intact
Expressive aphasia. Inferior frontal gyrus (Brodmann 44/45). Patient understands but cannot produce fluent speech. Repetition impaired. Aware of deficit → frustrated.
Inferior Division
temporal + inf. parietal
Lower MCA branches. Wernicke's aphasia (dominant). Superior quadrantanopia. Hemispatial neglect + anosognosia (non-dominant). Less motor deficit.
Wernicke's Aphasia
fluent · poor comprehension
Receptive aphasia. Post. sup. temporal gyrus (Brodmann 22). Fluent but paraphasic speech, word salad. Cannot understand. Patient unaware of deficit.
Deep / Lenticulostriate
perforating branches
Basal ganglia, internal capsule, corona radiata. Lacunar infarcts. Small vessel disease (HTN/DM). No cortical signs — no aphasia, no hemianopia, no neglect.
Pure Motor Hemiplegia
PLIC · face=arm=leg
Most common lacunar syndrome. Posterior limb internal capsule. Equal face, arm, leg involvement. No sensory loss. Classic HTN-related small vessel disease.
```

Ischaemic Pathology Cascade

Causative factor
Atherosclerosis, embolism, small vessel disease → arterial occlusion.
→
ATP depletion
CBF falls below threshold (~20 mL/100g/min). Oxidative phosphorylation fails within minutes of complete ischemia.
→
Na⁺-K⁺ ATPase failure
Pump failure → cytotoxic edema. Intracellular Na⁺ and water accumulate. Cell swells. DWI restriction appears within 30 min.
→
Glutamate release
Excitotoxicity. NMDA receptor activation → Ca²⁺ influx. Triggers proteases, lipases, endonucleases. Spreads to penumbra.
→
Ca²⁺ entry
Intracellular Ca²⁺ activates destructive enzymes. Mitochondrial dysfunction, ROS production, caspase activation → apoptosis.
→
Liquefactive necrosis
Brain undergoes liquefactive (not coagulative) necrosis — due to high lipid content + proteolytic enzymes from microglia. Cavitation on imaging weeks later.

Acute Management

IV tPA (Alteplase)
≤4.5h · 0.9mg/kg
10% bolus + 90% over 60 min. Max 90 mg. BP target <185/110 before, <180/105 during. CI: recent surgery, INR>1.7, plt <100k, ICH.
Thrombectomy
LVO · ≤24h (DAWN/DEFUSE)
M1/M2 occlusion. NIHSS ≥6. Stent retriever or aspiration. Extended to 24h with perfusion mismatch imaging. Door-to-puncture <90 min target.
Aspirin 325mg
24h post-tPA or if no lysis
Do NOT give with tPA. Within 24–48h otherwise. Reduces early recurrence by ~25%.
Permissive HTN
Allow ≤220/120
Impaired autoregulation. Lowering BP can extend penumbra infarct. Only treat if >220/120 (no lysis) or for tPA eligibility. IV labetalol or nicardipine.
Goals of Rx
acute stroke
1. Revascularization & reperfusion. 2. Avoid secondary injury. 3. Establish collateral blood flow. Complications at 4–5 days: ↑ICT, SIADH, metabolic issues, hemorrhagic transformation.
